Research

What I've Discovered

NLP · Deep Learning · Machine Learning · UT Arlington · CSE-6363
Fine-Grained Emotion Detection in Text using DistilBERT
Traditional sentiment models collapse the richness of human emotion into three buckets. This research asks a harder question: can a model detect that someone is simultaneously nervous and excited? Fine-tuned DistilBERT — 40% smaller and 60% faster than BERT — on 58,000 annotated Reddit comments from the GoEmotions dataset, framing it as a true multi-label classification problem across 8 consolidated emotion categories.
Three methodological innovations drove the result: class-balanced loss weighting ensured rare emotions like fear and disgust weren't drowned out by dominant categories; dual learning rates (conservative for pre-trained layers, aggressive for the classification head) prevented catastrophic forgetting during fine-tuning; and per-emotion threshold calibration let each class find its own optimal precision-recall trade-off. Micro-F1 of 0.8339, Macro-F1 of 0.81 — an 81% relative improvement over the published GoEmotions BERT baseline, with near-equal performance across all emotion categories confirming fairness alongside accuracy.
